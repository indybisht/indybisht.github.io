indira bisht

Writer. Director. Maker of Worlds.

about

Indira Bisht is a Mumbai-based writer and filmmaker known for developing character-driven commercial stories with strong emotional and psychological layers. Her screen credits include Khoobsurat (2014), Hiccups & Hookups (2021), and Netflix's Class (2023). She began her professional life in corporate branding before transitioning into creative work. Her early career in brand communication laid the groundwork for a storytelling sensibility rooted in identity, image, and storytelling.

Between 2006–2007, she contributed to two seasons of Sesame Street India for Miditech, working on long-format children's storytelling for this iconic American brand. Around 2011, she worked with Disney UTV in script development, on mainstream Hindi film projects during a formative phase of studio-led content expansion in India. Running parallel to her screen work, she has also worked as a business journalist, for a branding magazine, where she interviewed The Body Shop founder amongst other business heavy weights.

Indira holds a Master's degree in Politics from Jawaharlal Nehru University (JNU), along with a degree in Branding and Communication from MICA Ahmedabad. Her academic background informs her interest in identity, class, gender, and contemporary Indian social structures within mainstream cinematic narratives.

As a screenwriter, she has sold a feature script to Little Town Films, the production company behind Tumbbad. She is currently developing Dalhousie — an unraveling of a couple's marriage as their start-up rolls to success — with filmmaker Anu Menon, a project presently being pitched to studios and platforms.

In parallel, Indira is in post-production on her independently produced and directed short film It's All in the "I", centered on an out-of-work Bombay actress who survives by mimicking ordinary women — an exploration of identity.

Her work explores identity, hierarchies, performance, class, loneliness, and "people becoming someone" within contemporary Indian life, often through emotionally layered characters navigating morally ambiguous worlds. She is particularly interested in portraying women beyond simplistic cultural archetypes, with authentic psychological honesty and complexity.
